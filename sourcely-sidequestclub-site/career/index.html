Career minimalism: what if the ladder isn't the goal?

For a long time, success had one shape: get a job, work hard, get promoted, keep climbing. A lot of Gen Z isn't buying it. Not because they're lazy, but because they want a different return on their time.

The ladder, and who still wants the top

Deloitte's 2025 Global Gen Z and Millennial Survey asked more than 23,000 people in 44 countries about their goals. Only 6% said reaching a leadership position was their main career goal.

That doesn't mean they've stopped caring. It means the top of the ladder, with its titles, bigger teams and higher pay, isn't the only prize on the table.

What career minimalism means

In August 2025, Gen Z career expert Morgan Sanner described “career minimalism” in Glassdoor. Her picture is a career lily pad instead of a ladder: you hop to whichever opportunity fits your life right now.

It does not mean doing the bare minimum at work. It means work stops being the center of everything. Instead of maximizing prestige forever, you pick something else to maximize:

  • autonomy
  • meaning
  • flexibility
  • learning
  • creativity
  • a life outside of work

ambition didn't shrink. it just stopped pointing only up.

What it looks like in real life

There's no single version. A career minimalist might:

  • change industries
  • take a break, or go back to school
  • build a side business
  • freelance
  • choose a job on purpose because it leaves room for health, family or creative work

On paper, that path can look messy. But a zigzag can still have a thread running through it: your curiosity, your values, the kind of problem you keep wanting to solve.

Try it this week

  1. Write down the one thing you'd most like to maximize right now. Just one.
  2. List what your current path gives you toward it, and what it costs.
  3. Pick one small experiment, like a side project or a skill you'd like to learn, and give it a few hours.

Statistics are from Deloitte's 2025 Global Gen Z and Millennial Survey. Career minimalism and the lily pad idea are from Morgan Sanner in Glassdoor, August 2025. Opinion piece, not career or financial advice.
